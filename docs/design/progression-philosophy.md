# Progression Philosophy

## Build Evolution

A build is more than increasing damage.

A build represents the player's decisions throughout the run.

Weapons, passive items, NPC relationships, modules, and transformations should all contribute to build identity.

---

## Character Transformations

Character appearance should evolve naturally throughout progression.

Visual changes are not purely cosmetic.

Every major transformation should have meaningful gameplay consequences.

Possible consequences:

- new resistances
- new weaknesses
- altered melee attack
- NPC reactions
- unique dialogue
- area access
- boss interactions
- alternative endings

The player's appearance should tell the story of their journey.
Transformation is specialization, not progression.

---

## Affinity System

Transformations should not rely solely on collecting a fixed number of items.

Instead, every Path may have an Affinity value.

Examples:

Technology

Faith

Nature

Corruption

Memory

Progress

Identity

Player actions, equipment, NPC decisions, and important events may all influence Affinity.

---

## Transformation Stages

Each Affinity may evolve through multiple stages.

Example:

Human

↓

Minor Changes

↓

Partial Transformation

↓

Advanced Transformation

↓

Complete Transformation

NPCs and the world may react differently depending on transformation stage.

---

## Hybrid Builds

Players are not restricted to a single Path.

Multiple Affinities may coexist.

Examples:

Technology + Corruption

Faith + Nature

Radiation + Progress

Future systems may support unique hybrid transformations.

Hybrid identities should encourage experimentation rather than optimization.

---

## Replayability

Replayability should emerge naturally.

Reasons to replay include:

- different NPC choices
- different Paths
- different transformations
- alternative endings
- hidden bosses
- rare items
- hybrid builds
- unexplored lore

Players should replay because they remain curious about the world.

---

## Long-Term Goal

The player's journey should gradually answer one question:

"What have I become?"

Not only mechanically,

but visually,

narratively,

and philosophically.

The final character should feel like the accumulated consequence of hundreds of meaningful decisions rather than a collection of random upgrades.
