# NPC System

## Purpose

NPCs are not only quest givers or merchants.

They are living characters that inhabit the world, each with their own
goals, beliefs, personalities and motivations.

The player's relationship with NPCs should become one of the major
progression systems of the game.

Every important decision made by the player should have the potential
to influence one or more NPCs, changing future interactions, available
content and the overall state of the world.

NPCs should feel like they exist independently of the player rather than
waiting for the player to interact with them.

---

# Design Philosophy

The world should feel alive.

NPCs should exist for reasons beyond serving the player.

Every important NPC should have:

- personal goals
- personal beliefs
- personality
- allies
- rivals
- opinions about other NPCs
- opinions about the player's actions

Instead of asking:

"What quest should this NPC give?"

The design process should always begin with:

"What is this NPC trying to accomplish?"

Quests should naturally emerge from those goals.

The player is not the center of every character's existence.
NPCs should continue pursuing their own ambitions regardless of whether
the player decides to help them.

---

# NPC Identity

Every major NPC should have a clearly defined identity.

An NPC should be recognizable not only by appearance but also by:

- dialogue
- motivations
- rewards
- gameplay mechanics
- relationship with other NPCs

Example

Blacksmith

Identity

A master craftsman trying to restore the Ancient Forge.

Goals

- collect rare metals
- recover forgotten blueprints
- rebuild legendary craftsmanship

Rewards

- weapon improvements
- module slot expansion
- crafting

---

Scientist

Identity

A researcher fascinated by mysterious Void energy.

Goals

- study unknown artifacts
- understand the Void
- develop experimental technology

Rewards

- experimental weapons
- technological upgrades
- unique weapon modules

---

Both NPCs may require the same artifact.

The player must decide who receives it.

There should rarely be an objectively correct decision.

Each choice should create different opportunities rather than better
or worse outcomes.

---

# NPC Relationships

NPCs should interact with each other, not only with the player.

Examples

Blacksmith dislikes Scientist.

Scientist respects Explorer.

Merchant secretly supports Smuggler.

Helping one NPC may improve or damage relationships with others.

NPC relationships should create meaningful decisions instead of obvious
"correct" choices.

Different NPCs may compete for the same resources, locations or artifacts.

The player should sometimes be forced to choose whose goals deserve
their support.

---

# Living World

NPCs should not exist as isolated systems.

Every important NPC should be capable of influencing the goals,
opinions and behaviour of other NPCs.

The player should often witness the consequences of helping one
character through changes in several others.

Example

Player supports Scientist.

↓

Scientist discovers Void technology.

↓

Blacksmith becomes frustrated because ancient craftsmanship is being
replaced by experimental machinery.

↓

Merchant notices increasing demand for technological equipment and
starts selling new products.

↓

Explorer refuses to enter the newly opened Void region until it
becomes better understood.

A single player decision should therefore create a chain of reactions
throughout the world.

NPC relationships should evolve over time.

Characters may become:

- allies
- rivals
- business partners
- enemies
- mentors

These relationships may naturally change depending on the player's
actions.

The player should feel that NPCs continue living their own lives,
even when the player is occupied elsewhere.

The world should never feel like it exists only while the player
is looking at it.

---

# Reputation

The game should avoid a traditional morality system.

Instead of:

Good

Bad

The player develops individual reputation with different NPCs
and factions.

Examples

- Blacksmith Reputation
- Scientist Reputation
- Explorer Reputation
- Merchant Reputation
- Void Reputation

High reputation may unlock:

- new dialogue
- exclusive quests
- discounts
- unique rewards
- hidden lore

Negative reputation may:

- close quest lines
- increase prices
- remove certain upgrades
- unlock different opportunities

No reputation path should be considered the "correct" one.

Every path should offer unique gameplay and encourage replayability.

---

# Dialogue System

Dialogue serves three primary purposes.

1.

Reveal character personality.

2.

Reveal world lore.

3.

Allow the player to make meaningful decisions.

Dialogue should remain concise.

NPCs should gradually reveal information across many encounters instead
of explaining everything immediately.

Dialogue choices may influence:

- reputation
- future conversations
- quest availability
- rewards
- world progression

NPC conversations should help players become emotionally attached to
the characters instead of serving only as information delivery.

---

# Quest Philosophy

Quests exist because NPCs have goals.

They should never feel like random tasks.

Examples

The Blacksmith requests ancient metal because he wants to restore
the Ancient Forge.

The Scientist searches for Void crystals because she believes they
can unlock forgotten technology.

The Explorer maps dangerous ruins to uncover lost history.

The Merchant searches for profitable trade opportunities.

Each completed quest should move an NPC closer to or further away from
their personal objective.

---

# Branching Quest Lines

Important quests should present meaningful choices.

Example

Recover Ancient Crystal

↓

Give it to Blacksmith

OR

Give it to Scientist

↓

Different rewards

Different quest lines

Different NPC relationships

Different future events

Different endings

The player should rarely experience every quest line in a single run.

Replayability should naturally emerge from player decisions.

Some quest lines may also unlock different areas of the world.

Example

Help the Scientist.

↓

Gain access to the Void.

OR

Help the Blacksmith.

↓

Unlock advanced weapon improvements.

Choosing one path often means delaying or sacrificing another.

The player should constantly evaluate which opportunities are most
valuable for their current build and long-term goals.

---

# World Progression

NPC decisions should permanently influence the world.

Examples

The Blacksmith restores the Ancient Forge.

↓

New weapon upgrades become available.

The Scientist unlocks Void technology.

↓

A new biome becomes accessible.

The Explorer discovers forgotten ruins.

↓

Additional locations become available.

The player should clearly see that the world evolves over time.

Returning to familiar locations should reveal visible changes caused
by previous decisions.

---

# Long-Term Progression

NPC progression may continue across multiple game runs.

Examples

- unlock new NPCs
- expand the central hub
- unlock new crafting options
- unlock new weapon families
- unlock additional biomes
- unlock new bosses

Relationships with NPCs should become one of the permanent progression
systems outside individual runs.

The player's decisions should slowly shape the world across many runs,
making every playthrough part of a larger journey.

---

# Design Goals

The NPC system should:

✔ Make the world feel alive.

✔ Encourage exploration.

✔ Reward curiosity.

✔ Create meaningful choices.

✔ Increase replayability.

✔ Avoid obvious "good" or "bad" decisions.

✔ Support multiple playstyles.

✔ Encourage emotional attachment to characters.

✔ Make player decisions feel meaningful.

✔ Allow the world to evolve over time.

NPCs should become memorable because of their personalities,
their ambitions and the consequences of interacting with them,
not simply because they sell useful items or assign quests.
