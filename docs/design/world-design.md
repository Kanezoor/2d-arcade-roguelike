# World Design

## Vision

The world is not a collection of disconnected levels. It is a collection of interconnected ideas presented through different Paths.

Each Path explores a philosophical question about humanity. The environment, enemies, NPCs, bosses, music, mechanics, and visual style should all support the central theme of that Path.

Examples (subject to change):

- Path of Progress
- Path of Faith
- Path of Nature
- Path of Corruption
- Path of Memory
- Path of Freedom
- Path of Identity

The purpose of a Path is not to present a correct answer, but to encourage the player to think about the question it raises.

---

## Multiple Worlds

Different Paths may exist in entirely different locations.

Examples:

- abandoned planets
- underground civilizations
- parallel universes
- forgotten dimensions
- artificial worlds
- impossible spaces

Different Paths are not required to share the same history or even the same physical laws.

---

## World Identity

Every Path should have its own:

- architecture
- atmosphere
- environmental storytelling
- enemy families
- NPC factions
- soundtrack
- hazards
- mechanics

A player should immediately recognize which Path they entered without reading any text.

---

## The Player

The player is NOT a chosen one.

The player is simply another traveler.

NPCs already have their own lives before the player appears.

The player's choices influence the world, but the world does not revolve around the player.

This philosophy should be reflected throughout the game's writing.

---

## Hidden Content

Secrets should be an essential part of exploration.

Examples:

- hidden rooms
- breakable walls
- alternate entrances
- environmental puzzles
- throwable-only passages
- NPC hints
- secret bosses
- hidden endings

Players should feel rewarded for curiosity rather than completion percentage.

---

## Long Runs

Runs are intended to be significantly longer than those found in many traditional roguelikes.

Each run should gradually evolve into its own story.

The player's build, relationships, discoveries, and transformations should become increasingly unique as the run progresses.

---

## Hub Philosophy

The game does not begin with a permanent hub.

Instead, safe locations should be discovered naturally throughout exploration.

Different hubs may belong to different Paths and civilizations.

Unlocking a hub should feel like discovering a meaningful place within the world rather than entering a mandatory menu area.

---

## World Persistence

The world should support persistence whenever technically possible.

Possible future goals:

- resume interrupted runs
- reconnect after multiplayer disconnects
- preserve discovered hubs
- remember NPC states
- remember important world events

The implementation details will depend on future networking architecture.
